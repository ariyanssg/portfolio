## The problem

A business that sells access to software accounts has to track a lot by hand: which products it offers, which real account sits behind each one, how many seats are left on a shared account, which customers renew when, and who needs help. Spreadsheets break as soon as more than one person uses them.

## What I built

A CRM with two sides, built on Next.js and Supabase.

**Admin console**

- Dashboard and analytics: active subscriptions, customers, revenue and plans about to expire.
- Products, either shared with a seat limit or individual, each with a price and billing cycle.
- Master accounts: the real accounts behind each product. Their passwords are encrypted with AES-256 before they are stored, and only admins can decrypt them.
- Subscriptions: assign customers to an account with a live check of remaining seats, add several customers at once by email, and export the list to PDF.
- Customers, support issues with internal-only comments, and in-app notifications.

**Customer portal**

- The products a customer has, with renewal dates.
- Support issues they can open and follow.
- Notifications and profile settings.

## Decisions

- **Security lives in the database.** Every table has row-level security policies, so a customer can only ever read their own rows, whatever the front end does.
- **The database keeps the history.** Triggers write audit logs and notifications as data changes.
- **Renewals run on a schedule.** A Supabase Edge Function extends subscriptions automatically and is protected by a secret.

## Stack

Next.js (App Router), React, TypeScript, Tailwind CSS, shadcn/ui on Radix, React Hook Form and Zod, Supabase (PostgreSQL, Auth, row-level security, Edge Functions) and jsPDF.
